# Voices for pre-rendered radio shows

Status: spike, 2026-11-06. Licence facts below were read from the model cards, dataset cards and
licence texts on the dates stated; anything I could not read is marked **unverified**. This is an
engineering classification, not legal advice. Re-check before shipping: model cards change.

Songs are the user's own and are played by the app; only the talk is pre-rendered by
`scripts/render-shows.py` using voices assigned in `content/voices.json`. Models are never committed
(`.gitignore` covers `render-out/` and `.render-cache/`; models live outside the repo).

## Recommendation in one paragraph

Use **Kokoro-82M v1.0 (Apache-2.0)** as the main engine and **Piper voices whose whole lineage is
public domain or CC BY** (Cori, Kristin, John, Norman, LJSpeech, LibriTTS-high) as a supplement.
Do **not** ship any Piper voice that was fine-tuned from `lessac` or `ryan`, which is most of the
Piper English catalogue, until the lessac/ryan licence position is resolved (see "Lineage problem").

## Licence classes

| Class | Meaning |
|---|---|
| OK | Model and dataset licences permit commercial redistribution of generated audio with no attribution duty found |
| OK+attr | As OK, with an attribution duty (CC BY etc.); add to in-app credits |
| NC | Noncommercial only (a licence term I read says so) |
| UNCLEAR | Licence text not found, "See URL" that I could not resolve, or base model lineage is NC/research-only. **Do not ship.** |

## Kokoro-82M (recommended main engine)

Model: https://huggingface.co/hexgrad/Kokoro-82M (README front matter `license: apache-2.0`);
ONNX conversion used in the spike: https://huggingface.co/onnx-community/Kokoro-82M-v1.0-ONNX
(`license: apache-2.0`, base_model hexgrad/Kokoro-82M, revision 1939ad2a, modified 2025-02-08). Checked 2026-11-06.

Training data (author's statement in the README, **not independently verifiable**): "exclusively on
permissive/non-copyrighted audio data": public domain, Apache/MIT-licensed, and synthetic audio generated by
closed TTS providers (no open-TTS or voice-clone output). The README lists CC BY audio that was in the
training set: Koniwa `tnc` (CC BY 3.0, Japanese, <1 h) and SIWIS (CC BY 4.0, French, <11 h). Neither is English,
but add a credit line to be safe. Residual risk: synthetic data from closed TTS vendors may be under those
vendors' terms; the author asserts it is fine. Voices are style vectors shipped under the same Apache-2.0 release;
the README gives no separate per-voice licence.

Class for all voices below: **OK (Apache-2.0), with a credit line recommended; training-data provenance is the
author's claim (unverified)**.

## Piper voices (English), full classification

Source of every row: the voice's `MODEL_CARD` at
`https://huggingface.co/rhasspy/piper-voices/resolve/main/en/<en_GB|en_US>/<name>/<quality>/MODEL_CARD`
(all 38 English cards fetched 2026-11-06) plus the dataset licence pages I could read. The repo-level tag
`license: mit` on rhasspy/piper-voices does **not** override the per-voice dataset terms stated on each card.

### Lineage problem (why most Piper voices are not recommended)

* **lessac**: dataset "Lessac Technologies Blizzard 2013". Its licence (read at
  https://www.cstr.ed.ac.uk/projects/blizzard/2013/lessac_blizzard2013/license.html) grants "Research Purposes only" and
  explicitly excludes "any commercial purpose, including the development, marketing, commercialisation, sale or licencing
  of voice synthesis ... products or services", non-sublicensable. Class **NC**.
* Most other Piper voices say "Finetuned from U.S. English lessac voice (medium quality)". Whether a fine-tune of a
  model trained on research-only data inherits that restriction is unsettled; I class them **UNCLEAR** (do not ship)
  even where their own dataset is CC BY / CC0.
* **ryan**: RyanSpeech, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. Class **NC**. Voices "Finetuned from ... Ryan voice (low quality)" are
  **UNCLEAR** for the same reason.

| en_US-libritts-high (spk 5) | 0.27 | 22050 | n/a |

LibriTTS-high: 48 speakers sampled (every 19th id), one line each: F0 <150 Hz for 26, >=170 Hz for 16, in between 6
(median-F0 gender guess; I did not read the corpus speaker metadata, /resources/60/speakers.tsv returned 404). So the
904-speaker space holds hundreds of voices of each gender, but they are audiobook-reading style, quality varies per
speaker, and I could not audition any.

Piper peak-normalises every synthesised chunk to full scale (each chunk has exactly one full-scale sample); that is
not clipping, and the pipeline's loudness stage removes level differences. **Piper output is not reproducible**:
the same text synthesised twice gave different WAVs (VITS noise), hence the content-hash cache in the pipeline.

## Do these give 12-16 host voices and 6-8 callers?

By counts, yes, with the caveat that "usable" is judged by the Kokoro author's grades and my F0 spread, not by ear:

* Hosts (grade >= C or lessac-free Piper), about 16: US female af_heart, af_bella, af_nicole, af_sarah, af_aoede,
  af_kore; UK female bf_emma, bf_isabella, Piper cori; US male am_michael, am_fenrir, am_puck, Piper john, Piper norman;
  UK male bm_george, bm_fable.
* Callers (can be rougher: the phone-line EQ hides defects): the D-grade Kokoro voices (am_echo, am_liam, am_eric,
  am_onyx, af_jessica, af_river, bf_alice, bm_daniel, bm_lewis), Kokoro blends, pitch-shifted copies of host voices,
  and any number of LibriTTS-high speakers.
* Gaps: there is no age control in either engine. "Older" must be faked with a lower voice (am_onyx, bm_lewis,
  am_fenrir), a pitch factor around 0.92-0.95, a slower speed and the phone treatment. There is no non-US/UK accent
  (no Australian, Irish, etc.) among licence-clean English voices; UK male is Kokoro only.

## Pipeline notes

Setup (what I ran; engines in a venv outside the repo):

```
python3 -m venv /path/venv && /path/venv/bin/pip install kokoro-onnx piper-tts
# models, each in its own directory outside the repo (layout expected by --models / $RUNOUT_VOICES_DIR):
#   piper/<voice>.onnx + piper/<voice>.onnx.json         from huggingface.co/rhasspy/piper-voices
#   kokoro/model.onnx + kokoro/voices/<name>.bin          from huggingface.co/onnx-community/Kokoro-82M-v1.0-ONNX (onnx/model.onnx, voices/*.bin)
/path/venv/bin/python scripts/render-shows.py --dry-run
/path/venv/bin/python scripts/render-shows.py --out render-out --only tape-hiss-fm
```

github.com release downloads (where `kokoro-onnx` normally fetches models) are blocked in this sandbox (403), which is
why the Hugging Face ONNX mirror is used.

Loudness: target -19 LUFS integrated, measured with ffmpeg `loudnorm` (analysis pass), gain applied with `volume`, then
`alimiter` at -2 dB sample peak. The encoded files re-measured -19.2 to -19.9 LUFS on the fixture (the limiter and Opus
account for the 0.2-0.9 LU shortfall); the measured value is stored per segment in the manifest (`lufs`).

Size (Opus mono, libopus, VBR, application voip): measured 7.3 / 10.6-11.1 / 14.0 MB per hour of speech at 16 / 24 /
32 kbps on short fixture segments (theory: 7.2 / 10.8 / 14.4). AAC-LC at 24 kbps measured 12.1 MB/h (container overhead on
short files). Tape Hiss FM dry-run estimate: 69 renderable segments, ~53 min of speech, ~10 MB at 24 kbps; the estimate runs
about 10% high against the fixture. 12 of its 81 segments use slots and cannot be pre-rendered.

Broadcast treatments are data in `content/voices.json` (`phone`, `am-radio`, `lofi-tape`); verified to run and to band-limit
(`phone` caller: about 0.7% of energy above 3.4 kHz in the rendered Opus), tuned by numbers only, never by ear.
